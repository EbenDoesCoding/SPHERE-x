Planet X & SPHEREx

Explore a patch of sky as a sequence of infrared images. Changes in position or brightness can reveal objects worth investigating.

SPHEREx surveys the sky in 102 infrared bands, revisiting it every six months. This viewer connects that changing sky to curious people.

The five challenge targets

Barnard’s Star, Epsilon Indi B, WISE 0855, RW Aurigae and R Draconis span moving sources and variable stars.

Designed for exploration

Choose coordinates and field of view; play, scrub or compare frames; inspect detectors, wavelength groups and contamination metadata; add Gaia positions and share a coordinate link.

Epoch stacking is supported by the backend. Spectra, calibrated light curves, WISE overlays and scientific difference imaging are future work.

Know what you’re looking at.

The M101 offline sequence contains three real FITS observations downloaded directly from IRSA and NASA’s public S3 mirror using file paths in the official SPHEREx tutorial. The unmodified backend renderer aligned them onto a common sky grid, with one shared greyscale stretch and no oversampling. Their wavelengths differ; this is an image-access test, not a motion or variability measurement. The JSON export records download URLs, checksums, timestamps and processing details.

Repository samples are PNGs committed in SPHERE-x’s test folder. Their dates and detector IDs come from filenames; wavelengths are recorded in its offline test. Full calibration, quality flags and provenance are not available for these files. They are for testing the viewer, not confirming a discovery.

Live mode displays backend metadata without inventing missing values. Shared sky grids and stretches are handled by the backend. “All wavelengths” may mix colours and cause apparent variability. Compare matching wavelengths and at least two epochs.

Gaia positions use proper motion; the backend does not apply parallax. Missing Gaia markers do not establish a new object. WISE 0855 is not represented by a Gaia track. Image artefacts and incomplete coverage need scientific review.

Sources: SPHERE-x backend and samples · IRSA mission and detector information. Independent hackathon frontend; not an official NASA product.
